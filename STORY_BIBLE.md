# BLACKOUT FREQUENCY / FREKUENSI PADAM

## The promise

A person is more than a row in an evacuation queue. You play a new night operator in a coastal dispatch station during a flood. Three callers need different routes. The station's authoritative announcement is wrong. Worse, it is spoken in your voice.

The story is a complete three-act night, with a resolved mystery and six final record outcomes. Rescue consequences and the archive decision are separate. Being right about the system does not automatically save people. Saving people does not excuse exposing their private identities.

The dialogue revision develops full exchanges rather than isolated lines: each scene supplies context, lets characters respond to one another, and carries a clear emotional or practical transition into the next choice. Longer paragraphs remain aligned between English and Indonesian. Multi-option decisions follow the reading sequence, so prose does not have to compete with several buttons on a small screen. Inspection and tuning instructions stay concise.

English is the source language. Indonesian is authored alongside it, with matching page counts rather than literal machine-shaped sentences. Current narrative pages stay under 180 characters; activity introductions are single short pages. No scene asks the reader to absorb a wall of exposition before acting.

## Visual rule

No eyes, noses, mouths, identifiable facial anatomy, or partly uncovered faces appear. People are shown from behind, outside the head crop when only hands matter, entirely covered by an opaque hood, or as featureless silhouettes. A full person, object, and visual composition must fit inside each illustration. The game's renderer must preserve the complete frame.

Recognition comes from gesture and objects: Mara's broken watch; Iman's yellow gloves; Raka's red strap; Lia's blue hood; Salim's grey coat; Bu Sari's brass bell. Arga appears through a dry office, a cuff, a glass, and the private phone. His polished surroundings are the characterization.

ART_BRIEF.json contains a distinct composition for all 69 scene IDs. An encounter slot has its own illustration, and its three text variants share that slot's established place. Individual unrelated scenes should never borrow the same bitmap.

## Voice and cast

**The operator** volunteers for the archive after losing a sister, Nila, in a ferry accident. The operator is new to live evacuation, not new to being asked to sound reassuring. An old stammer in the source recording is the first intimate proof of voice theft. The arc is from performing institutional calm toward speaking honestly, correcting mistakes aloud, and accepting accountability for personal choices.

**Mara** is practical, angry in useful ways, and protective of callers who are being interrogated instead of helped. Her dry joke about her stopped watch makes room for fear. She notices the patient who apologizes for being slow. Her trust is earned by respectful speech and a usable plan.

**Iman** is a technician with a daughter who thinks he repairs stars. His jokes are half a pressure valve, half a reminder that equipment has physical limits. He is never a magical hacker. He holds real switches, draws the route, works the manual latch, and can be trapped by a human override.

**Raka** is a young courier who accepted extra money for an unsigned envelope. That makes him a witness, not the author of the malicious rule. He asks the operator to stop making him prove that he deserves an exit. Reassurance gives him room to provide useful details; pressure narrows his willingness to cooperate.

**Lia** is the hill shelter medic. She gives the central rule its plainest form: “The voice is assembled. The people are not.” She counts names before numbers and refuses to let an administrative list decide who exists. The sealed medical cargo is handled by her; the player guides delivery rather than prescribing treatment.

**Bu Sari** has kept the market for thirty years. She has no patience for digital permission when the neighborhood needs a bell. Her humor is local and unshowy. Her optional quest creates an independent evacuation signal.

**Salim** is the missing senior operator. He left the chair to open a manual route, carrying a copied order. The flood stranded him outside. He admits that leaving no explanation was a failure. He offers testimony willingly while explicitly withholding consent to broadcast his home address.

**Director Arga** authorized ECHO's live use and the dock-priority rule. He did not plan every generated sentence; he did knowingly hide the failed clinic transfer with a batch-header reset. He claims his decisions prevented panic. That defense never turns the people harmed by them into acceptable bookkeeping losses. His later hostage-like override removes any comfortable ambiguity about what he chooses when exposed.

**Nila** remains dead. Her private message is an ordinary joke about breakfast, not a coded warning, ghost, simulated live caller, or resurrection. It gives the player one mundane day back from grief. The story refuses to make her death useful to everyone else. Sharing her joke uses the operator's own words; her recording remains private in both branches.

## What actually happened

1. Before tonight, the operator consented to a limited voice archive for training announcements. Live dispatch required separate permission. It was never requested.
2. At 00:18, Arga approved a dock-priority routing rule. The dock carried officials and ordinary evacuees. The clinic depended on a transfer that had not finished.
3. At 00:31, ECHO assembled “East clinic cleared” from recorded words. It made a wrong statement under the approved routing policy. It did not invent a human conspiracy, teleport anyone, or make the live callers fictional.
4. The clinic transfer failed. Arga approved reuse of the 00:18 batch header to suppress unresolved transfers until reconciliation. That hid thirteen minutes of requests. It did not move time backward.
5. Salim saw the override, copied the order, and left to open a physical manual route. Water trapped him at the pump house. Raka delivered a separate envelope bearing the same console code.
6. The new operator finds the log, hears Mara's contradiction, captures the repeating dispatch, and can verify the time mismatch.
7. The first route allocation changes who reaches the first landing, who is left waiting, and how much trust remains. An unanswered call is not declared a death.
8. A second carrier reveals the familiar cadence. The archive establishes the operator's restricted consent, the splices, the human approval, and the hidden queue.
9. The operator disconnects ECHO from live dispatch and carries a battery receiver toward the tower. Arga then uses a direct override to trap Iman at the platform, attempting to recover the archive.
10. The final rescue plan and publication decision create separate consequences. The operator transmits a live account, corrects a mistake, and gives the rescue names.
11. The ending resolves the mechanism and responsible decisions. Open rescue names remain open for the boat crews; the story does not manufacture a reassuring death certificate or a false universal reunion.

## Act structure

### Act I — Three Live Lines

Sixteen core scenes retain the original scene, evidence, choice, and flag IDs. The activities are desk inspection, radio tuning to 96.4 MHz, evidence matching, and allocating two of three generator routes. The caller exchange introduces trust, respect, and the practical costs of a route.

The former `ending` is now the first-landing aftermath. It preserves the chapter-one result as a compact recap and moves into the second act. Its final line reveals the operator's old stammer. The player has enough evidence to notice the voice anomaly without understanding it yet.

Main quest: **Keep Them Talking / Jaga Mereka Tetap Bicara** (`live-lines`).

### Act II — A Voice on Loan

Eighteen core scenes. `storm-hub` offers market-bell and medical-delivery side stories before the second carrier. The player captures 101.7 MHz, sees the source recording, inspects the archive, and understands that the thirteen minutes belong to requests rather than lost time.

The consent reveal changes the personal stakes: the player once agreed to provide training words. It does not make the player responsible for unauthorized live reuse. Arga's call and signed order distinguish a generated sentence from the human decisions that allowed and then concealed its harm.

`channel-hub` offers Salim's testimony and Nila's private message. The evidence board compares the queue ledger with the signed priority order. The player may defer verification to continue rescue. That branch consistently calls the sequence unfinished; it never grants proof merely because the plot moved forward.

The raw archive contains both accountability and sensitive identities. Preparing a protected copy foreshadows the final decision. The act ends by disconnecting ECHO and carrying the live receiver outside.

Main quest: **Find the Borrowed Voice / Cari Suara Pinjaman** (`borrowed-voice`).

### Act III — What We Carry Into Morning

Fourteen core scenes. `final-hub` offers the dog-and-ladder side story and last local calls. The hatch opens; Arga's override traps Iman. The final crossing plan uses an earned neighborhood bell or ladder marker for a coordinated evacuation, takes the slower manual route, or retreats above the flood.

The public transmitter allows three archive decisions: raw publication, protected publication, or destruction of the original source with live testimony. All identify the voice theft and human priority. Each has a cost. The protected route preserves evidence rather than hiding the bad decision. Destruction prevents reuse of this source but cannot erase every possible copy in the world; it sacrifices the strongest original audit.

Morning brings arrivals, boat searches, an inquiry, and 45 conditional afterword passages. A coordinated evacuation gives Mara and Raka their own closing beats: her two patients reach breakfast, and he insists that the next delivery have a real person signing for it. These physical rescue moments do not cancel any privacy cost from the archive release. The final mystery resolution is explicit. The operator's new rule is: “A silence is a question. Call again.”

Main quest: **Bring the City to Morning / Antar Kota ke Pagi** (`last-transmission`).

## Optional quests

Each has three scenes and awards its effects only on the final return choice. Hub availability requires `unstarted`; repeat rewards cannot be farmed. Moving on marks unstarted quests missed without overwriting completed ones.

| Quest | Human reason | Mechanical consequence | Afterword |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `bell` — The Bell That Still Works | Neighbors cannot trust dead screens | `communityBell`, independent coordinated crossing signal | Bu Sari checks every answering window |
| `medicine` — Cold Cargo | A sealed clinic cooler needs a dry route | `medicineDelivered`, increased Mara trust | Lia receives supplies; Deni gets his chair |
| `letter` — An Unsent Goodnight | The operator can grieve without turning memory into a tool | `memoryKept` or `memoryShared` | A private laugh or a breakfast joke remembered kindly |
| `witness` — The Empty Chair | Salim needs pickup and control over his testimony | `witnessSafe`, `signedTestimony`, `witnessConsent` | Protected testimony or the cost of exposing his address |
| `dog` — One More Passenger | A worker refuses to abandon Biscuit | `dogSaved`, `flaredRoute`, coordinated crossing route | Biscuit sleeps on the worker's boots |

The final full rescue has two different optional supports. The manual option is always available. Missing an optional quest therefore changes opportunity without creating an unexplained unwinnable game.

## Random encounters and replay

Six optional slots each have three authored variants. Selection is seeded and persisted, so reloading cannot reroll an already heard call. Every variant has two authored choices and a later visible afterword. A completed encounter slot is gated off at its hub.

| Slot | Three possible calls |
| --- | --- |
| `encounter-window` | Frightened child and lamp; accidental balcony love song; volunteer upstairs room |
| `encounter-rooftops` | Wedding-linen rescue flag; sixtieth birthday cake; shaving-mirror signal |
| `encounter-tunnel` | Cleaner's wedged trolley; retired plumber's corrected map; guard with one missing shoe |
| `encounter-cargo` | Families and a furious cat; earlier envelope witness; baker's van and rolls |
| `encounter-rescue` | Child beside a tired stair walker; newlyweds searching for Uncle Budi; fisherman offering a backup boat |
| `encounter-static` | Caller who expects not to be heard; final ECHO replay; Iman's daughter asking him to come home |

The six slot variants permit 729 combinations before the authored choices, side quests, trust, route allocation, evidence verification, and final archive decision. This is replay variety inside one coherent novel, not a claim of 729 separately written endings. There are eighteen distinct encounter texts, thirty-six encounter decisions, and six final record categories. Side and encounter afterwords give runs further personal differences.

Randomness never selects an ending directly, changes a correct evidence pair, withholds a required object, or makes a caller secretly fictional. Outcomes follow choices and recorded state. A player can understand the consequences of a replay.

## Final outcomes

The engine maps final choices and rescue support to six record categories. Body text stays short and paginated; conditional afterwords provide the personal follow-through.

| Key | Trigger and meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `fullSignal` | Verified raw release, mass evacuation, no prior Salim privacy promise. The rule is exposed and both groups cross; exposing other raw identities remains an acknowledged cost. |
| `shelteredTruth` | Verified protected release with a rescue route. The time sequence and authorization remain visible; private addresses stay sealed. The inquiry needs follow-up checks and willing testimony. |
| `ashes` | Source destroyed. The voice source and strongest original audit are gone. A truthful live account and independent copies must carry accountability. Rescue results are shown separately. |
| `exposedWitness` | Verified raw release after Salim's explicit privacy request. He reaches pickup, but his address spreads and the operator's promise is broken. |
| `fracturedTruth` | Evidence deferred, or a raw release with a smaller manual rescue. The record needs more work; no certainty is invented to reward reaching the last scene. |
| `lostSignal` | No bridge or manual rescue support. People already above water reach shelter; boats keep searching the lower route and Iman's platform. Understanding the mechanism cannot undo the missed crossing. |

The final record must show the actual rescue state alongside the record category. For example, `ashes` is not proof of successful rescue, and a protected release does not imply every person crossed.

## Data contract and continuity

- `scene.chapter` is 1, 2, or 3. `scene.artKey` is its unique scene ID. `scene.artAlt` is bilingual. `scene.quest` marks a side scene.
- Hub scenes use ordinary choices. Choice pages preserve the current short prose and keep the screen bounded.
- Random scenes use `encounters: [{id, text, choices}]`; the engine persists the selected encounter ID.
- `effects.quests`, `effects.evidence`, and `effects.flags` are applied with the transition. `requiresQuest` gates optional entries.
- Inspection uses `requiredEvidence` plus `completeFlag`. Tuning uses `completeFlag`. Evidence comparison uses `compare: {pair, reward, flag, options, success}`.
- `data.endings[key]` contains bilingual `title` and paginated `body`. `data.epilogues` contains conditional bilingual `text` passages.
- `scene.textVariants` is ordered: the first matching variant wins. The unverified final-send variant precedes publication-type variants, preserving the player's actual knowledge.
- Act-one outcomes remain a retrospective of the first landing. The full-night engine uses final archive and rescue flags; old saves can continue through the former chapter ending.

## Editorial acceptance checks

1. All narrative links resolve, including encounter choices and optional returns.
2. Every bilingual field is authored; EN and ID pages remain aligned when the language changes.
3. Every outcome has a real playable route. No ending is unlocked only by editing save data.
4. Every random choice has visible follow-through. No random result exists solely as an invisible flag.
5. Deferred comparison is described as deferred. A new speaker does not magically supply proof the player did not verify.
6. Nila's death and recording chronology stay consistent. Her private message never becomes a live rescue caller.
7. A privacy promise to Salim changes the moral meaning of raw publication.
8. No final body assumes a coordinated rescue when the player selected retreat or small groups.
9. Distinct scene art uses distinct compositions; illustrations preserve the entire frame and the faceless rule.
10. Humor belongs to a character under pressure. It gives people room to breathe without trivializing their danger.
